Title: Bhagavad Gita Knowledge Dataset

Overview:
The Bhagavad Gita, commonly known as the Gita, is a 700-verse Sanskrit scripture that forms a part of the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata (Book 6). It is presented as a dialogue between Prince Arjuna and Lord Krishna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra before the beginning of the great war between the Pandavas and Kauravas.

Historical Context:
Name: Bhagavad Gita (Song of the Lord)
Original Language: Sanskrit
Source Text: Mahabharata
Section: Bhishma Parva (Book 6)
Chapters: 18
Traditional Verse Count: 700 verses
Setting: Battlefield of Kurukshetra
Author Tradition: Traditionally attributed to Sage Vyasa as part of the Mahabharata
Composition Period: Scholars have different views; generally estimated between the last centuries BCE and early centuries CE.

Purpose:
The Bhagavad Gita explores questions about duty, ethics, human nature, the self, God, action, knowledge, devotion, and liberation.

Main Characters:

1. Arjuna:
- One of the five Pandava brothers.
- A highly skilled warrior and archer.
- Faces a moral and emotional crisis before fighting against his own relatives, teachers, and friends.
- Seeks guidance from Krishna regarding duty and righteousness.

2. Lord Krishna:
- Arjuna's charioteer and spiritual teacher.
- Reveals teachings about Dharma, Karma Yoga, Jnana Yoga, Bhakti Yoga, meditation, and liberation.
- Is presented as a divine manifestation and Supreme Being in many Hindu traditions.

3. Sanjaya:
- Advisor and charioteer of King Dhritarashtra.
- Receives divine vision from Sage Vyasa to witness and narrate the events of the Kurukshetra battlefield.

4. Dhritarashtra:
- King of Hastinapura.
- Father of the Kauravas.
- Blind from birth and concerned about the outcome of the war.

5. Pandavas:
- Five brothers: Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva.
- Represent the side associated with Dharma in the Mahabharata.

6. Kauravas:
- The hundred sons of Dhritarashtra.
- Led by Duryodhana.
- Opponents of the Pandavas in the Kurukshetra war.


Core Philosophical Teachings:

1. Dharma (Duty):
Dharma refers to righteous duty, moral responsibility, and actions aligned with justice and harmony.
Krishna teaches Arjuna that he must perform his duty as a warrior while avoiding selfish attachment.

2. Karma Yoga (Path of Selfless Action):
Karma Yoga teaches performing one's responsibilities without attachment to personal outcomes.
The focus should be on sincere action rather than the desire for rewards.

Key Principle:
One should focus on performing the right action and not become controlled by attachment to results.

3. Jnana Yoga (Path of Knowledge):
Jnana Yoga emphasizes understanding the true nature of the self.
The Atman (soul) is described as eternal, while the physical body is temporary.

4. Bhakti Yoga (Path of Devotion):
Bhakti Yoga teaches devotion, love, and surrender to the divine as a path toward spiritual realization.

5. Dhyana Yoga (Path of Meditation):
The Gita explains meditation, discipline, concentration, and control of the mind.


Important Concepts:

Atman:
The eternal self or soul that is distinct from the temporary physical body.

Brahman:
The ultimate reality or universal consciousness described in Vedantic philosophy.

Dharma:
The principle of righteousness, duty, and moral order.

Karma:
The principle that actions have consequences.

Moksha:
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death and realization of the ultimate truth.

Yoga:
A spiritual discipline or path for achieving union with higher consciousness.

Gunas:
The three qualities of material nature:
- Sattva: Purity, knowledge, harmony
- Rajas: Activity, desire, passion
- Tamas: Inertia, ignorance, darkness


The 18 Chapters of Bhagavad Gita:

Chapter 1:
Arjuna Vishada Yoga (The Yoga of Arjuna's Grief)
- Arjuna observes both armies and becomes overwhelmed by sorrow and moral confusion.

Chapter 2:
Sankhya Yoga (The Yoga of Knowledge)
- Krishna explains the immortality of the soul and introduces the concept of selfless duty.

Chapter 3:
Karma Yoga (The Yoga of Action)
- Explains the importance of performing duty without attachment.

Chapter 4:
Jnana Karma Sannyasa Yoga (Knowledge and Renunciation of Action)
- Explains divine knowledge, spiritual wisdom, and Krishna's teachings about incarnation.

Chapter 5:
Karma Sannyasa Yoga (The Yoga of Renunciation)
- Discusses the relationship between action and renunciation.

Chapter 6:
Dhyana Yoga (The Yoga of Meditation)
- Explains meditation, concentration, and controlling the mind.

Chapter 7:
Jnana Vijnana Yoga (Knowledge and Realization)
- Explains knowledge of the divine and understanding Krishna's nature.

Chapter 8:
Akshara Brahma Yoga (The Yoga of the Imperishable Absolute)
- Discusses Brahman, remembrance of God, and the nature of existence.

Chapter 9:
Raja Vidya Raja Guhya Yoga (The Yoga of Royal Knowledge and Secret)
- Describes the highest spiritual knowledge and devotion.

Chapter 10:
Vibhuti Yoga (The Yoga of Divine Manifestations)
- Describes Krishna's divine qualities and manifestations throughout creation.

Chapter 11:
Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga (The Vision of the Universal Form)
- Krishna reveals his universal cosmic form to Arjuna.

Chapter 12:
Bhakti Yoga (The Yoga of Devotion)
- Explains devotion as a direct path toward spiritual realization.

Chapter 13:
Kshetra Kshetrajna Vibhaga Yoga (The Field and Knower of the Field)
- Explains the difference between the body and the conscious self.

Chapter 14:
Gunatraya Vibhaga Yoga (The Division of Three Qualities)
- Explains sattva, rajas, and tamas.

Chapter 15:
Purushottama Yoga (The Yoga of the Supreme Person)
- Explains the relationship between the individual self and the Supreme Being.

Chapter 16:
Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga (Divine and Demonic Qualities)
- Discusses positive and negative qualities in human behavior.

Chapter 17:
Shraddhatraya Vibhaga Yoga (Three Types of Faith)
- Explains different forms of faith influenced by the three gunas.

Chapter 18:
Moksha Sannyasa Yoga (The Yoga of Liberation and Renunciation)
- Summarizes Krishna's teachings about duty, knowledge, devotion, and liberation.


Major Themes:

1. Duty and Responsibility:
The importance of performing one's responsibilities according to Dharma.

2. Self-Control:
Managing desires, emotions, and the mind.

3. Detachment:
Performing actions without being controlled by attachment to results.

4. Spiritual Knowledge:
Understanding the difference between the temporary body and eternal self.

5. Devotion:
Developing a relationship with the divine.

6. Liberation:
Achieving freedom from ignorance and the cycle of rebirth.


Important Verses:

Chapter 2, Verse 47:
Krishna teaches the principle of performing one's duty without attachment to the results.

Chapter 2, Verse 20:
The soul is described as eternal and not destroyed with the destruction of the body.

Chapter 4, Verse 7-8:
Krishna explains the purpose of divine incarnation for protecting Dharma.

Chapter 18, Verse 66:
Krishna teaches surrender to the divine as the highest path toward liberation.


Major Commentators:

Adi Shankaracharya:
- Associated with Advaita Vedanta philosophy.
- Interprets the Gita through the perspective of non-dualism.

Ramanujacharya:
- Associated with Vishishtadvaita Vedanta philosophy.
- Emphasizes devotion and the relationship between individual souls and God.

Madhvacharya:
- Associated with Dvaita Vedanta philosophy.
- Emphasizes distinction between individual souls and the Supreme Being.
